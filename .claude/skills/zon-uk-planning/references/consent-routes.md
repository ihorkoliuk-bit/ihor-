# Consent routes (England)

| Route | Use when | Cannot | Statutory determination |
|---|---|---|---|
| Outline permission | Establish principle and quantum (units, use, access) | Fix detailed design unless applied for | 13 weeks major (10+ homes or site ≥0.5 ha), 8 weeks minor |
| Reserved matters (RM) | Approve appearance, landscaping, layout, scale (and access if reserved) within the outline | Change the description of development, the number of units, the use class, or breach outline conditions/parameter plans | 13 weeks major. Must be submitted within the time limit on the outline (normally 3 years) |
| Full planning | New scheme or anything the outline description does not cover | — | 13 weeks major |
| S73 (vary/remove a condition) | Change a condition, e.g. approved plans | Change the description of development (Finney v Welsh Ministers [2019] EWCA Civ 1868) | 13 weeks major |
| S96A non-material amendment | Very small change judged non-material | Anything the council considers material | 28 days |
| Pre-application advice | Test the scheme with officers before submitting | Bind the committee | Fee and timing set by the council |

Practical points:
- "Up to 25 bungalows" permits single-storey dwellings up to 25 units. Two-storey houses, rooms in the roof (depending on wording), extra units or a commercial use need a new full application.
- Conditions labelled "pre-commencement" must be discharged before any works start; build them into the programme (typically 8 weeks each, often submitted together).
- Commencement: a lawful material start before the permission expires keeps it alive.
- Committee vs delegated: a major scheme with objections usually goes to committee; add 4–8 weeks.
- Judicial review window after decision: 6 weeks. Lenders often wait it out.
