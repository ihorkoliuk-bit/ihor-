# UK space and accessibility checks

Checks to run on every plan for England. Report pass/fail with the number, not just "compliant". These are summaries; the architect confirms against the current documents.

## Nationally Described Space Standard (NDSS, 2015)

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1b1p | 39 (37 with shower only) | – | – | 1.0 |
| 1b2p | 50 | 58 | – | 1.5 |
| 2b3p | 61 | 70 | – | 2.0 |
| 2b4p | 70 | 79 | – | 2.0 |
| 3b4p | 74 | 84 | 90 | 2.5 |
| 3b5p | 86 | 93 | 99 | 2.5 |
| 3b6p | 95 | 102 | 108 | 2.5 |
| 4b5p | 90 | 97 | 103 | 3.0 |
| 4b6p | 99 | 106 | 112 | 3.0 |
| 4b7p | 108 | 115 | 121 | 3.0 |
| 4b8p | 117 | 124 | 130 | 3.0 |

Room rules:
- Double/twin bedroom ≥ 11.5 m², ≥ 2.75 m wide (main double ≥ 2.75 m; other doubles ≥ 2.55 m).
- Single bedroom ≥ 7.5 m², ≥ 2.15 m wide.
- At least 75 % of the GIA at ≥ 2.3 m floor-to-ceiling height. Areas under 1.5 m headroom do not count.
- A room counted as a bedroom must meet the bedroom minimum; a study smaller than 7.5 m² is not a bedroom.

Bedspaces: count 2 per double bedroom and 1 per single. A "2-bed" with one double and one single is 2b3p.

## Accessible and adaptable dwellings — Part M4(2)

Key checks that show on a plan:
- Step-free approach and entrance; entrance door clear opening ≥ 850 mm.
- Internal doors clear opening ≥ 750–800 mm depending on corridor width (900 mm corridor → 800 mm door).
- Hallways ≥ 900 mm clear (≥ 1050 mm where turning into a door).
- WC at entrance level; space for a future level-access shower.
- 1500 mm turning circle in living spaces; 1000 mm clear zone beside the bed on one side of the main bedroom.

## Measurement conventions (RICS)

- GIA: to the internal face of external walls, including internal walls, stairs and voids per storey footprint.
- NIA: usable area; excludes walls, columns, and in houses typically not used for sales.
- GEA: to the external face; used for planning and some cost plans.
- Sales particulars and valuations use GIA of habitable space. Garages, terraces, porches and car ports are listed separately.

## Bungalow vs. two-storey

A consent for "bungalows" means single-storey dwellings. Rooms in the roof (chalet bungalow) may or may not be accepted depending on the wording and conditions. Flag any two-storey type placed on a bungalow consent.
